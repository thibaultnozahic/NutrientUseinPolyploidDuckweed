# Nutrient Use in Polyploid Duckweed

Polyploidy, or possessing more than two sets of chromosomes, is common in plants. When new polyploids form, they emerge surrounded by diploids, thus competing with them for nutrients. For neopolyploids to establish, they need to coexist or be more competitive than their diploid progenitors for limiting nutrients. Whether ploidy shifts nutrient use parameters, and whether such differences permit coexistence under nutrient limitation and thus establishment rather than competitive exclusion remains unresolved. Here we compare nutrient-use between diploid and colchicine-induced neotetraploid Spirodela polyrhiza. Nutrient-omission batch cultures and one-day batch cultures enabled us to use the Droop-Monod model, a system of non-linear ODEs. With Bayesian statistical inference we determined the nutrient use parameters per ploidy level and/or nutrient to then determine a hypothetical coexistence niche between diploid and tetraploid duckweed. Minimal nutrient requirements were greater in tetraploids for both nutrients. Mortality was higher in tetraploids when nitrogen was omitted but equal to the diploid when phosphorus was omitted. Growth rates were higher in diploids compared to tetraploids. Nutrient consumption parameters were found to be equivalent between ploidy levels with slight differences for nitrogen. We discovered coexistence to be very unlikely between diploid and tetraploid because the tetraploid was limited for both nutrients.
